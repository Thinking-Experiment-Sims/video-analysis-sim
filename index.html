💡 Why Linearize Parabolic Motion?

In physics, fitting a parabola \(y(t) = rac{1}{2}at^2 + v_0t + y_0\) contains three dependent parameters \((a, v_0, y_0)\). Simply graphing \(y\) versus \(t^2\) does not produce a straight line unless the initial velocity \(v_0 = 0\).

\[y(t) = rac{1}{2}at^2 + v_0t + y_0\]

🎯 The Discovery Method: Avoiding Dependence on v₀

How can students isolate acceleration \(a\) without needing \(v_0 = 0\) at the release point?

Look at the apex (peak) of the position-time curve! At the apex \((t_{ ext{apex}}, y_{ ext{apex}})\), the instantaneous vertical velocity is exactly zero: \(v_y(t_{ ext{apex}}) = 0\).

Shifted Vertex Form:
\[[y - y_{ ext{apex}}] = rac{1}{2}a(t - t_{ ext{apex}})^2\]
Let \(Y' = [y - y_{ ext{apex}}]\) and \(X' = (t - t_{ ext{apex}})^2\): \[Y' = ( ext{Slope}) \cdot X' \quad ext{where} \quad ext{Slope} = rac{1}{2}a \implies a = 2 \cdot ( ext{Slope})\]
